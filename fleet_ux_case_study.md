# Fleet Charging Management - UX Case Study

## Overview

Fleet Charging Management is an experience designed for teams who manage electric vehicle fleets, depot charging, vehicle readiness, and daily trip schedules.

From a UI/UX design point of view, my main focus was to make the product feel less like a data-heavy operations tool and more like a clear decision-making workspace. Fleet managers do not simply need information; they need to know what needs attention, why it matters, and what action to take next.

The improved experience was shaped around one core question:

**"What needs attention right now, and what should I do next?"**

The UX direction focused on clarity, urgency, quick comparison, and smoother movement between high-level fleet health and detailed vehicle or depot decisions.

## The Challenge

Fleet charging operations involve many moving parts at the same time. A manager has to keep track of vehicle battery levels, upcoming trips, depot availability, charging delays, costs, and unexpected alerts.

The main UX challenge was that this kind of information can quickly become overwhelming. If everything is shown with the same priority, the user has to spend too much time figuring out what is actually important.

Key problems to solve:

- Managers needed a faster way to understand fleet readiness at the start of the day.
- Urgent vehicles had to be easier to identify without scanning every row manually.
- Charging issues needed to be shown with clear priority and context.
- Users needed to understand whether a vehicle was safe for its next trip or at risk.
- Depot and charging information had to feel connected to the overall fleet plan.
- The experience needed to support both quick decisions and deeper investigation.

The goal was to reduce confusion and help users move from awareness to action with less effort.

## The Key Solution

The improved UX was designed as a fleet control workspace, where the most important decisions are visible first and every detail supports a clear next step.

### 1. A clearer dashboard for instant understanding

The dashboard was redesigned around operational priorities rather than general statistics. The top area highlights the most important daily signals:

- Vehicles that need charging now
- Trips that may be at risk
- Expected energy cost
- Expected downtime cost

This helps the manager understand the condition of the fleet within a few seconds. Instead of presenting all data equally, the interface brings urgent information to the front.

### 2. Priority-based vehicle visibility

Vehicles are organized by urgency, making it easier to see which ones need attention first. Battery condition, next trip readiness, and risk indicators are shown in a simple visual format.

This improvement reduces the need for manual checking and supports faster decision-making during busy operations.

### 3. Map-based operational awareness

A map view was added to connect vehicle status with location. This helps managers understand whether charging issues are isolated, depot-related, or spread across the fleet.

The map supports a more natural way of thinking:

- Where is the vehicle?
- Is there a nearby depot?
- Is the nearest charger available?
- Is this issue urgent because of location or timing?

This makes the experience feel more practical and grounded in real-world operations.

### 4. Charging schedule as a planning workspace

The charging schedule was shaped as a visual timeline so users can understand the day at a glance. Instead of reading long lists of sessions, managers can see when each vehicle is charging, where gaps exist, and where conflicts may happen.

The schedule supports three important views:

- Past activity, to understand what actually happened
- Today's plan, to compare completed work with upcoming sessions
- Future planning, to prepare vehicles before issues appear

This creates a stronger sense of control across past, present, and future operations.

### 5. Conflict handling with guided action

Charging conflicts are shown as moments that need attention, not as hidden errors. The experience explains why an issue matters and gives the user practical options, such as choosing a faster charger, reducing waiting time, or adjusting the plan.

This turns problem-solving into a guided flow instead of making the user diagnose everything alone.

### 6. Role-focused experience

The experience separates the needs of fleet managers and depot managers. Fleet managers focus on readiness, risk, and planning. Depot managers focus on charger availability, active sessions, and local depot operations.

This keeps each user focused on the decisions they are responsible for and avoids unnecessary complexity.

## Impact

The improved UX creates a calmer and more action-oriented experience for fleet charging teams.

Expected user impact:

- Faster understanding of daily fleet readiness
- Less time spent searching for urgent vehicles
- Clearer visibility of trips that may be affected
- Better connection between vehicle status, depot status, and charging plans
- Reduced decision fatigue during high-pressure operations
- More confidence when adjusting schedules or responding to alerts
- Easier communication between fleet and depot teams

The biggest improvement is that the product no longer feels like a collection of separate screens. It feels like one connected workflow where the user can move from a high-level issue to the right action quickly.

## Learnings

This project reinforced that operational products need more than clean layouts. They need strong information priority.

Important UX learnings:

- In complex dashboards, users should not have to search for what matters most.
- Visual urgency helps users act faster, but it must be used carefully to avoid stress.
- A good operations experience should move from overview to detail naturally.
- Timeline views are powerful when users need to understand time-based planning.
- Alerts become more useful when they are connected to clear actions.
- Different user roles should not see the same experience if their decisions are different.

The main learning was that UX improvement in fleet operations is not about adding more information. It is about helping users trust the information, understand the priority, and take the next step with confidence.

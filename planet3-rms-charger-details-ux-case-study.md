# Planet3 RMS Charger Details - UX Case Study

## Overview

This case study focuses on the Charger Details experience in the Planet3 Remote Management System, created from scratch to help teams monitor and manage EV chargers with clarity.

My role in this project was primarily as a Business Analyst and Product Owner. I worked on understanding how information moves from the charger to the system, what each user group needs to know, and how that information should be presented in a way that supports quick decisions.

The goal was to create a practical, user-friendly experience for operations teams, service teams, site managers, and business stakeholders. Instead of simply displaying charger data, the product had to explain the current condition of a charger, highlight what needs attention, and help users decide the next action.

## The Challenge

The biggest challenge was turning complex charger behavior into a simple and useful product experience.

An EV charger produces many kinds of information: live status, connector availability, session activity, alerts, maintenance needs, connectivity status, energy performance, and site-level context. For users, this information is only valuable if it is organized in a way they can understand and act on.

As a BA and Product Owner, I had to bridge the gap between how the charger communicates with the system and how users expect to experience that information on the screen.

Key challenges included:

- Understanding what charger information matters most to users.
- Defining how charger status, connector status, alerts, sessions, and maintenance should be connected.
- Creating a clear structure for users with different levels of responsibility.
- Avoiding information overload on a data-heavy screen.
- Making sure users could identify problems without needing deep technical knowledge.
- Translating system behavior into product language that is easy to understand.

The product needed to support both high-level monitoring and deeper investigation, without making the experience feel complicated.

## The Key Solution

The solution was to design the Charger Details experience around user decisions.

I defined the page structure so users could first understand the overall health of the charger, then move into specific areas like connector performance, sessions, alerts, and maintenance when needed.

The experience starts with the most important information: energy delivered, successful sessions, faulted sessions, charger health, connection status, and service-related details. This gives users a quick understanding of whether the charger is working well or needs attention.

The charger section was planned as a visual health view, where important details are placed around the charger image. This helps users connect information to the physical charger and makes the experience easier to scan.

I also separated charger-level information from connector-level information. Charger-level information explains the condition of the overall machine, while connector-level information focuses on each charging port. This separation creates a clearer mental model for users and reduces confusion.

Main product and UX decisions included:

- Creating a clear overview-first experience.
- Prioritizing key health indicators at the top of the page.
- Separating charger health from connector performance.
- Grouping sessions, alerts, and maintenance in a way that supports user workflows.
- Using simple status language so non-technical users can understand the screen.
- Highlighting warnings and faults so users can identify issues faster.
- Creating a flow from "What is happening?" to "What should I check next?"
- Designing the experience to support both monitoring and troubleshooting.

The focus was not only on what information should appear, but also on why it matters to the user and where it should appear in the journey.

## Impact

The Charger Details experience gives users a clearer and more actionable way to monitor EV chargers.

Expected product and UX impact:

- Faster understanding of charger condition.
- Easier identification of faults, warnings, and service needs.
- Better clarity between overall charger health and individual connector status.
- Reduced effort for operations teams during daily monitoring.
- Improved communication between support teams and field service teams.
- Better visibility into session performance and charger reliability.
- A more approachable product experience for users who do not need deep technical detail.

By defining the experience from a product owner perspective, the page became more than a data view. It became a decision-support screen that helps users understand charger behavior, identify issues, and take the right next step.

## Learnings

This project helped me understand how important the BA and Product Owner role is in shaping a useful product experience.

A strong product experience starts with understanding how the system works, but it becomes valuable only when that understanding is translated into a clear user journey. In this project, learning how the charger communicates with the system helped me define what users should see, how they should interpret it, and how the page should guide them.

Key learnings:

- Product clarity begins with understanding the real system behavior.
- A BA must translate complex information into simple user-facing flows.
- A Product Owner needs to prioritize what users need first, not just what data is available.
- Separating information by user context makes the experience easier to use.
- Operational products need clear hierarchy because users often work under time pressure.
- Good UX is not only visual design; it is also structure, language, priority, and decision flow.

The final experience reflects a product-first approach: understand the charger, understand the user, and then shape the interface around meaningful action.
